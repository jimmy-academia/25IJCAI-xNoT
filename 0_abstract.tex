
\begin{abstract}
Large language models struggle to reason over structured, multi-source data: existing prompting methods conflate heterogeneous evidence and remain vulnerable to adversarial content in user-generated text.
We introduce \textbf{ANoT} (\textbf{A}daptive \textbf{N}etwork \textbf{o}f \textbf{T}hought), a split-context prompting method that decomposes reasoning into isolated, verifiable steps across three phases---Planning, Adaptation, and Execution---with three-valued logic aggregation for explicit uncertainty handling.
On a Yelp-based recommendation benchmark with adversarial attacks, ANoT outperforms 13 baselines by +16\% on clean data; under attack, baselines degrade by \textbf{XX\%} while ANoT drops only \textbf{YY\%}.
\end{abstract}
