\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in applications that require reasoning over \emph{structured, multi-source data}---information combining rich metadata with unstructured content from multiple contributors~\citep{chen2025survey}.
Consider a recommendation system: to match a user's query (``quiet cafe with free WiFi, open Monday afternoon, where reviews mention good matcha''), an LLM must jointly reason over structured attributes, temporal constraints, and dozens of customer reviews.
Such scenarios are ubiquitous---from e-commerce product search to healthcare record analysis to legal document review---yet existing prompt schemes provide inadequate support.

This setting presents three interrelated challenges.
\emph{First}, multi-source data introduces heterogeneity and adversarial vulnerability: user-generated content exhibits uneven quality, and real-world platforms are susceptible to fake reviews, prompt injection attacks, and coordinated manipulation~\citep{greshake2023not}.
\emph{Second}, structured text requires distinct handling from unstructured text; LLMs struggle to jointly process hierarchical metadata alongside free-form content without conflating evidence types~\citep{zhao2024struceval}.
\emph{Third}, complex user requests involve compositional logic with nested conditions, requiring models to correctly parse operator precedence and propagate uncertainty across subexpressions.
These difficulties are pervasive in practice, yet no existing prompt scheme adequately addresses their combination.

Existing prompting methods fall into two categories based on how they handle input data.
\emph{Full-context schemes}---including Chain-of-Thought~\citep{wei2022chain}, ReAct~\citep{yao2022react}, and Least-to-Most~\citep{zhou2022least}---process all available information in a single LLM call.
While effective for homogeneous text, these methods degrade on structured multi-source data: they conflate evidence from different sources and prove vulnerable to adversarial content that exploits the shared context.
\emph{Split-context schemes}---such as Decomposed Prompting~\citep{khot2022decomposed}---process information in separate calls, achieving better modularity.
However, existing split-context methods still fall short: they lack content-aware adaptation to detect attacks before execution, provide no explicit evidence tracking across sources, and do not support compositional logic aggregation with uncertainty handling.

We propose \textbf{\methodfull (\method)}, a split-context prompt scheme that addresses all three challenges through a principled three-phase architecture.
\textbf{Phase 1 (Planning)} decomposes the user request into structured conditions mapped to specific evidence sources---this explicit decomposition prevents conflation between metadata and unstructured content.
\textbf{Phase 2 (Adaptation)} analyzes actual data content to detect heterogeneity or potential attacks \emph{before} the main reasoning phase, ensuring adversarial content never reaches the decision logic.
\textbf{Phase 3 (Execution)} processes each evidence source independently and aggregates results using three-valued logic that explicitly handles uncertainty---enabling correct compositional reasoning while preserving minority viewpoints.

We evaluate \method on a structured recommendation benchmark constructed from Yelp Open Dataset, featuring 50 user requests across 10 complexity categories that vary by evidence type and logical structure.
We further test robustness against eight attack types, including prompt injection and fake reviews.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\method}, a split-context prompting method achieving high accuracy on compositional multi-source tasks with robustness through architectural isolation.
    \item A \textbf{structured recommendation benchmark} with complex queries and comprehensive attack evaluation.
    \item Empirical findings showing \method outperforms 13 baselines by +16\% on clean data while maintaining performance under attacks.
\end{itemize}
